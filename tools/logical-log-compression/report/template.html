Logical Log Compression

How well 16 MiB chunks of the change-streamer CDC stream compress — measured on real logical replication streams over Chinook, Pagila and zbugs — and what that means for S3 cost and restore time.

Method

What was actually measured

The bytes under test are the real thing. The harness starts a Postgres logical replication stream through initializePostgresChangeSource, drives real mutations, and serializes every data-plane message with serializeChangeStreamData — the same call sqlite-change-log-writer makes when it persists the log. Messages are framed as newline-delimited JSON and cut into 16 MiB chunks at message boundaries.

      Two things would otherwise flatter the numbers, and are deliberately
      avoided. Generated rows recombine sampled column values at co-prime

      real length distribution sampled from the seeded tables —
      cycling zbugs' 924 real comment bodies to fill a 16 MiB chunk would
      have made it look far more compressible than production traffic.
    

  


  

    

      
Results

      
Ratios span two orders of magnitude

    

    

      Every row is one 16 MiB chunk compressed with zstd-3. A
      row change is one insert, update or delete — not one message, since
      a single-row transaction emits three. The number that transfers to your
      own schema is stored bytes per row change.
    

    

  


  

    

      
Analysis

      
Where the bytes go

    

    

      Ratio is governed almost entirely by how much of the chunk is structure
      rather than payload. Every data message carries a full copy of its table's
      relation block, and every transaction is wrapped in
      begin/commit messages carrying LSNs, an xid and
      a commit timestamp.
    

    

    

    

    

  


  

    

      
Attribution

      
What each column costs on S3

    

    

      Removing one column from a chunk and recompressing gives that column's
      marginal cost. It separates “the row is wide” from “the
      row contains something incompressible” — the two have very
      different prices. Shares do not sum to 100%: this is marginal cost, and
      columns interact inside the compressor's window.
    

    

  


  

    

      
Codec

      
Picking the operating point

    

    

      Median across all 24 chunks, single-threaded, Node 22's built-in
      zlib bindings. Throughput is what a change-streamer would
      sustain on one core — and it is worth noticing up front how much
      headroom there is: even a shard writing 100k row changes a second only
      produces tens of MB/s of logical log.
    

    

    

  


  

    

      
Sizing

      
Chunk size, and the RPO trap

    

    

      A bigger chunk gives the compressor more history to fold the repeated
      structure into, so it pays off exactly where there is structure
      to fold. Where the payload is already near its entropy floor —
      deletes, or pagila's uuid-bearing rows — chunk size makes almost no
      difference. Nothing here argues for going below 16 MiB.
      (zstd-3, to isolate the size effect from the window setting.)
    

    

    

    

  


  

    

      
Cost

      
S3 at steady state

    

    

      S3 Standard in us-east-1: $0.023/GB-month, $0.005 per 1,000
      PUTs, $0.0004 per 1,000 GETs. Transfer to same-region EC2 is free. Seven
      days of retained logical log, at three sustained row-change rates.
